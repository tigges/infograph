## Picture 2 — China copy

### China · Point 1 · Agricultural / geographical features + population

> “The heartland is the political, cultural, demographic and — crucially — the agricultural centre of gravity.”
>
> **Hu Line** → “This imaginary line is probably the most important geographical feature in this study.”
>
> It explains the disparity of China’s population, and how China developed in the way it did. It speaks to the importance of agriculture. Shows how terrain and climate determined the development.

*(Marshall’s wording is the heartland / North China Plain. The Hu / Heihe–Tengchong Line is the student’s extra frame for the same east–west population split.)*

### China · Point 2 · Borders / buffer zone

> It acts as a buffer zone in the border regions to protect [the core] from instability and vulnerability.
>
> Large geographical space means they border many [states]. Without buffer zones [or a desert to the north?], it is easier to press inward.
>
> This is a by-product of the fertile east, and allows it to be protected. Some states are also used as more noted buffer zones. Respecting / repeating[?] in buffer zones are Mongolia [Tibet, Xinjiang].

*(Marshall: Gobi / Mongolia as an early-warning desert; Tibet, Xinjiang, Manchuria as buffers around the Han heartland.)*

### China · Point 3 · Navy + sea neighbourhood

> “A green-water navy patrols its maritime borders.” / “world’s largest navy”[?]
>
> China [is] closing about the size of a true sea [power], displaying naval dominance along the coastal area. It allows China to decide maritime trade routes, more exposure of natural resources, the ability to influence policy on a large scale as well as opening up employment in different sectors of the economy.

*(Marshall: “A green-water navy patrols its maritime borders, a blue-water navy patrols the oceans.” South China Sea / first island chain / blockade risk.)*

## Picture 3 — Africa copy

### Africa · Point 1 · Natural barriers (negative factor)

> Poor for transportation; waterfalls. Explains deficiency in technological / political development — in direct opposition to Western Europe, or even China.
>
> “These are just two in a long list of problems that help explain why Africa isn’t technologically or politically as successful.”
>
> Poor connection between North and South. Geography as a whole is a barrier. Trade infrastructure expensive (to the coast). Contrast to the Rhine. Trade routes ✕.

*(Marshall, Africa opening: “Great beaches… but terrible natural harbors. Rivers? … every few miles you go over a waterfall.” Sahara cuts north from south.)*

### Africa · Point 2 · Borders drawn due to colonisation + “Africa’s world war”

> “Many Africans are now partially the prisoners of the political geography the Europeans made.”
>
> Borders drawn without respect of ethnic geography — e.g. Sudan, Somalia — promoting political, social and economic instability.
>
> Contrast to China’s established unified core, protected by buffer zones, that has one set of natural buffers to certain threats.

*(Marshall: Europeans “drew lines on them — or… lies.” Ethnic conflict in Sudan, Somalia, DRC, Nigeria, etc.)*

### Africa · Point 3 · Population growth

> “Perhaps Africa’s biggest challenge, population growth” (p.217).
>
> “One in four people will be African.”
>
> An exploding population will need more infrastructure, increased production, and an agricultural sector to feed these people — but it is difficult to enforce contraception / a lower birth rate. Inevitable disparity may hold back development.

*(This population passage is in the student’s notes / later edition framing. The 2015 text stresses isolation, harbors, rivers, and colonial borders more than the 1-in-4 line.)*

## Comparison (from the sketch)

Similarities and differences, set larger than the point cards, answering: **why did one develop further?**

1. **Core** — Fertile, unified heartland vs isolating barriers
2. **Borders** — Chosen buffers around a Han core vs colonial lines that ignored ethnicity
3. **The sea** — A navy that can patrol home waters vs few harbors and rivers that cannot carry trade
